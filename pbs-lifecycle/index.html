PBS/MPS Architecture

The PBS Lifecycle

How the proposed Meritocratic Provenance System connects a documented human contribution with verifiable performance, contribution attribution, an economic claim, and continuing participation in the value created.

Follow the lifecycle What is PBS?

The central question

How does a contribution become an economic interest?

A contribution does not become economically meaningful merely because someone claims to have created value. The proposed system requires a documented contribution, evidence of measurable performance, a defensible method of attribution, and a defined legal or contractual mechanism for economic participation.

The PBS lifecycle organizes those requirements into a transparent sequence. Each layer answers a different question, and each layer creates evidence needed by the next.

The short version

MPS documents who contributed what, observes what measurable outcome occurred, estimates how the contribution relates to that outcome, and supports a proposed economic interest tied to the documented contribution and its attributable value.

End-to-end architecture

From human contribution to economic participation

The lifecycle below is a conceptual model. It describes the proposed functions of the architecture without asserting that a particular contribution automatically creates a security, property right, royalty, or legally enforceable claim.

Contribution
Provenance Record
Performance Evidence
Contribution Attribution
Economic Claim
PBS Instrument Layer
Royalty Participation
1

A contribution is made

What was contributed?

The lifecycle begins with a discrete or continuing input: expertise, intellectual work, data curation, creative work, an improvement, a diagnostic insight, a scientific result, an agricultural innovation, or another contribution capable of being documented.

For analytical treatment by the proposed system, the contribution must be identifiable, materially connected to downstream outcomes, and capable of having continuing influence.

2

The Provenance Ledger creates the evidence record

Who contributed what, when, and in what context?

The proposed Provenance Ledger records the contribution, contributor identity or verified identity reference, timestamp, supporting evidence, and relationships to prior contributions.

  • A contribution receives a distinct record.
  • Supporting evidence can be cryptographically anchored.
  • Prior contributions can be referenced to form a provenance chain.
  • Verification and dispute status can be preserved in the record history.
Explore the Provenance Ledger →
3

The Performance Oracle observes measurable outcomes

What happened in the real world?

The proposed Performance Oracle connects the documented contribution with domain-appropriate evidence. The relevant evidence depends on the field and may include model accuracy, agricultural yield, error reduction, robustness, replication, licensing performance, or another independently observable result.

Performance evidence does not, by itself, establish how much value belongs to any particular contributor. It establishes the observable outcome that the attribution process must explain.

Explore the Performance Oracle →
4

Contribution Decomposition estimates attribution

How did the documented contribution relate to the outcome?

Contribution Decomposition is the analytical bridge between the provenance record and the performance evidence. It estimates direct marginal contribution, interaction effects, and persistence over time.

  • Perturbation methods can estimate performance change when a contribution is masked, altered, or down-weighted.
  • Cooperative allocation methods can account for value produced jointly by multiple contributions.
  • Persistence modeling can represent continuing influence across time.
  • Confidence and known approximation error must remain visible.
Explore Contribution Decomposition →
5

An attributable economic claim is specified

What economic participation is supported?

Once attribution has been estimated, the relevant agreement or governing framework can specify the form of economic participation. That participation could involve a royalty, licensing interest, distribution right, or another contractually or legally defined claim.

The measurement system does not create legal rights by itself. The legal or contractual instrument must define the parties, scope, rights, limitations, dispute procedures, and enforceability of the claim.

6

The PBS layer represents the proposed economic interest

How is the interest documented or represented?

Provenance-Backed Securities are the proposed instrument layer connecting an economic claim with provenance-verified contributions or assets and related performance evidence.

Instrument form, transferability, governance, offering method, holder rights, and applicable law would affect the instrument’s legal classification and compliance requirements. This page does not assert that every contribution interest is a security or that every PBS would receive the same classification.

Read the public PBS overview →
7

Royalty and value flows remain linked to provenance

How does economic participation continue?

When the governing agreement provides for royalties or distributions, payment calculations can reference the documented contribution, validated performance evidence, and attribution methodology.

The proposed audit trail preserves the relationship among the original contribution record, subsequent performance evidence, attribution result, calculation, authorization, and payment history.

8

The lifecycle is reviewed and recalibrated

What happens when evidence changes?

Contributions can interact differently as systems evolve, performance can drift, and new evidence can affect prior estimates. The proposed architecture therefore treats attribution as an auditable and revisable process rather than a one-time declaration of permanent value.

Challenges, review, recalculation, recalibration, and updated distributions should remain documented so that changes do not erase the historical record.

Separation of functions

Three questions, three analytical layers

The architecture separates evidence, measurement, and attribution. Keeping these functions distinct helps prevent a performance result from being mistaken for proof of authorship or a provenance record from being mistaken for proof of economic value.

Layer 1

Who contributed what?

The Provenance Ledger creates the record of contribution, identity, evidence, time, and context.

Layer 2

What measurable outcome occurred?

The Performance Oracle gathers and validates domain-appropriate evidence of observable performance.

Layer 3

How are contribution and outcome related?

Contribution Decomposition estimates marginal impact, interactions, persistence, and attributable participation.

Proposed royalty-flow structure

Ri = α · PV(ci) + β · Σj ≠ i Syn(ci, cj)

In the proposed model, the royalty to contributor i combines a direct contribution component with an interaction component. The coefficients α and β are policy or contractual parameters, not naturally fixed constants.

Any implementation must also observe a budget-balance constraint: aggregate royalties cannot exceed the value available for allocation unless an identified external funding source is introduced.

Design principles

What the lifecycle is designed to preserve

A credible lifecycle must do more than produce an allocation. It must preserve evidence, expose uncertainty, support review, and distinguish technical integrity from legal validity.

Traceability

Each downstream calculation should remain connected to its originating contribution record and supporting evidence.

Measurability

Economic attribution should reference observable, domain-appropriate performance rather than credentials or unsupported assertions.

Epistemic humility

Attribution is an estimate. Confidence, approximation error, missing evidence, and alternative explanations must remain visible.

Auditability

Records, validations, calculations, challenges, recalibrations, and distributions should be capable of independent review.

Dispute handling

A participant must be able to challenge identity, evidence, performance data, attribution, or a resulting distribution through a defined process.

Incentive preservation

The framework is intended to reward demonstrated contribution while preserving useful market information and productive incentives.

Important distinction

What this lifecycle does and does not establish

The lifecycle is

  • A public explanation of a proposed architecture.
  • A separation of provenance, performance, and attribution.
  • A model for maintaining an end-to-end audit trail.
  • A framework for connecting measurable contribution with a separately defined economic interest.
  • A basis for technical, legal, policy, and institutional evaluation.

The lifecycle is not

  • Proof that every contribution creates a property right.
  • A promise that every recorded contribution has economic value.
  • A guarantee of royalties, appreciation, liquidity, or return.
  • A final legal classification of PBS under securities law.
  • Evidence of regulatory approval, endorsement, or technical validation.

Audit path

A reviewable chain from record to distribution

The proposed architecture is intended to let an authorized reviewer move backward from a payment or economic claim to the evidence that supported it.

1. Inspect the distribution Identify the payment, calculation, authorization, or economic claim under review.
2. Inspect the attribution Review the methodology, parameters, confidence level, and interaction treatment.
3. Inspect performance evidence Trace the result to the domain-specific observations and validation history.
4. Inspect provenance Return to the original contribution, identity reference, timestamp, context, and supporting evidence.
Public status note: The provenance, measurement, decomposition, instrument, and royalty functions described on this page are proposed design features. Actual implementation, enforceability, classification, and compliance would depend on the specific system, agreements, participants, governance, instrument terms, and applicable law.
