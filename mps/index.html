Primary-Source Explanation

What Is the Meritocratic Provenance System (MPS)?

The Meritocratic Provenance System is a proposed framework for documenting contribution, establishing provenance, incorporating measurable performance, and connecting documented contribution with attributable economic value.

What does MPS do?

MPS is designed to answer a fundamental question: when many people, organizations, datasets, ideas, or processes contribute to an economically valuable outcome, how can those contributions be documented and evaluated?

The framework uses provenance records to establish the history of contribution and independently measurable performance information to evaluate how those contributions relate to outcomes.

What is the core principle behind MPS?

MPS begins with the proposition that economic systems should be capable of recognizing documented contribution.

Provenance establishes who contributed. Performance measurement provides evidence about outcomes. Contribution analysis provides a framework for examining how individual and collaborative inputs relate to those outcomes.

What are the principal functions of MPS?

Function 1

Contribution Documentation

Creates records describing participants, contributions, authorship, development history, and relevant relationships between contributors and assets.

Function 2

Provenance

Establishes an auditable history describing where an asset or contribution originated and how the asset or contribution developed over time.

Function 3

Performance Measurement

Associates contributions with independently observable performance information relevant to the domain in which the asset operates.

Function 4

Contribution Attribution

Provides a framework for analyzing the relationship between documented contributions and measurable outcomes.

Relationship to PBS

How are MPS and PBS different?

MPS is the broader framework. It describes how contribution, provenance, performance, and attribution can be documented and analyzed.

Provenance-Backed Securities (PBS) are the proposed financial instrument layer that can connect economic claims with provenance-verified contributions or assets.

Put simply: MPS provides the framework; PBS provides a proposed instrument through which documented contribution may participate in economic value.

Technical Architecture

How does the MPS architecture work?

The public PBS/MPS architecture separates three related questions: what was contributed, what measurable outcome occurred, and how documented contribution relates to that outcome.

Layer 1

Provenance Ledger

Establishes the evidentiary record describing contributors, contributions, timestamps, evidence, and relevant provenance relationships.

Layer 2

Performance Oracle

Connects documented assets and contributions with domain-appropriate, independently observable performance information.

Layer 3

Contribution Decomposition

Examines the relationship between documented contributions and measurable outcomes, including situations in which contributions interact or persist over time.

Why does provenance matter?

Economic value can emerge from many contributors, prior works, datasets, processes, institutions, and accumulated forms of expertise.

A current market price or economic outcome does not, by itself, describe the causal history that produced that outcome. Provenance provides a framework for preserving and examining that history.

MPS therefore treats provenance as analytically important: before contribution can be evaluated, the contribution and its relationship to the resulting asset or outcome must first be documented.

Does MPS require perfect attribution?

No. The framework does not assume that complex economic or technical outcomes can always be decomposed with perfect certainty.

Contribution analysis can involve approximation, uncertainty, interacting inputs, changing conditions, and counterfactual questions about what would have happened without a particular contribution.

For that reason, the MPS research framework treats uncertainty as something that should be documented rather than concealed. Stronger conclusions should require stronger evidence.

Research Status

Is MPS an existing legal or regulatory system?

No. MPS is a proposed institutional, technical, and economic framework under continuing research and development.

The framework includes research concerning provenance, performance measurement, contribution attribution, economic participation, governance, and related legal and regulatory questions.

Descriptions of MPS on Meritocrat.org should therefore be read as descriptions of a proposed framework, not as statements that the framework has been adopted by a regulator, legislature, court, or other institution.
