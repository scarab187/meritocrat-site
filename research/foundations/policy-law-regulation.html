Foundation 6

Policy, Law and Regulation

MPS and PBS touch securities regulation, property and intellectual-property questions, contracts, privacy, data governance, and institutional design. This page distinguishes proposal from established legal status.

Public status rule: “proposed” means a design or policy concept; “author analysis” means analysis contained in project materials; neither means agency approval or a binding legal determination.

Core questions

What is PBS?

PBS is the proposed financial-instrument layer of the broader Meritocratic Provenance System. The source materials describe it as linking economic claims to provenance-verified contributions and performance data. That description is a proposal, not a final legal classification.

Is PBS necessarily a security?

No final classification is asserted here. The actual regulatory treatment would depend on the specific instrument terms, participants, governance, offering method, and applicable state and federal law.

What did Washington DFI actually say?

In the correspondence supplied for this project, DFI said the contemplated architecture went beyond the laws administered by DFI and could raise property-right and intellectual-property issues. DFI also said securities requirements could apply where an offer or sale of securities is involved, that securities offerings must be registered or exempt at the state and federal levels, and that it could not choose the appropriate regulatory pathway for the project.

Why does Washington’s risk-capital analysis matter?

The project’s own risk-capital report analyzes PBS under RCW 21.20.005(17)(a) and identifies investor managerial control as a central governance-dependent issue. That report is author analysis, not a DFI classification.

Why does governance matter?

The internal analysis treats voting rights, management replacement, operational authority, transaction vetoes, collective action, protocol upgrades, and control of fee or distribution structures as facts that could affect a control analysis. Final legal significance requires instrument-specific legal review.

What is the proposed Performance Oracle?

It is a proposed measurement layer that ingests domain-specific performance evidence and produces valuation signals. The design materials call for independent verification, anomaly detection, cross-validation, recalibration, and logged correction cycles.

What does cryptographic anchoring prove?

In the proposed design, hashes and signatures are used to make later modification detectable and to bind records to verified identities. Integrity of a record is different from truth of the original assertion, so external evidence, verification, and dispute procedures remain necessary.

How would attribution disputes be handled?

The design materials contemplate challenges, review, recalculation or recalibration, updated distributions, and logged dispute history. These are proposed mechanisms, not a statement of an existing statutory process.

Can contributors be pseudonymous?

The architecture contemplates verified but non-public identity modes. Any production implementation would still have to satisfy the privacy, identity, financial-regulation, and sector-specific rules that apply to the actual activity.

Is a “contribution right” already an established property right?

The project materials characterize contribution rights as novel and discuss contractual or legislative routes. This site should therefore treat “contribution right” as a proposed concept unless and until an applicable contract, statute, regulation, or binding decision gives it a specific legal status.

Is the proposed architecture already deployed?

The formal submission describes PBS/MPS as being at an architectural and intellectual-property development stage at the time of that submission. Public pages therefore describe ledger, Oracle, decomposition, royalty, identity, and examiner-access functions as proposed design features unless separately verified as implemented.

What is the practical legal takeaway?

The DFI correspondence strongly recommended competent counsel because the proposal is complex and may implicate multiple bodies of law. Public materials should distinguish research, proposed design, author legal analysis, and an agency’s actual statements.
