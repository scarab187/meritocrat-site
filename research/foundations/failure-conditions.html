Failure Conditions and Falsifiability

A framework becomes more credible when it identifies evidence that would count against it.

Interpretive rule: Failure in one domain does not automatically refute every provenance application. It may show that a method, metric, governance structure, or instrument is unsuitable for that domain.

Technical failure conditions

  • Reasonable attribution methods produce materially incompatible allocations from the same evidence.
  • Contribution estimates are unstable under modest changes to baseline, perturbation method, benchmark, or model specification.
  • Approximation error cannot be bounded tightly enough for the economic decision being made.
  • Interaction effects or retraining dynamics make individual attribution non-identifiable.
  • Oracle inputs can be manipulated more cheaply than the resulting economic benefit.

Economic failure conditions

  • The cost of documentation, verification, dispute resolution, valuation, and compliance consistently exceeds the value made available for allocation.
  • Royalty rules reduce useful contribution, collaboration, or adoption more than they increase it.
  • Secondary-market pricing becomes detached from performance evidence despite the proposed anchoring mechanisms.
  • Contributors cannot understand or challenge the rules affecting their allocation at reasonable cost.

Institutional and legal failure conditions

  • The system requires individual moral judgments that cannot be administered consistently or with due process.
  • The privacy burden of provenance reconstruction exceeds the public or participant benefit.
  • Governance concentrates effective control in parties whose interests conflict with contributors or holders.
  • The economic claim cannot be implemented through a valid contractual or statutory pathway without unacceptable investor, consumer, labor, or property-right risk.
  • A proposed correction produces greater arbitrary treatment than the condition it seeks to correct.

Required response to failure

A failed test should produce one of four responses: revise the method, narrow the domain, reduce the strength of the resulting claim, or suspend the application. Uncertainty should never be converted automatically into entitlement.
