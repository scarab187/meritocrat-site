Core Logic of the PBS/MPS Framework

Evidence, Attribution, Institutional Design, and Theoretical Boundaries

This hub exposes the reasoning that holds the broader provenance-based framework together. It distinguishes what is observed from what is inferred, what is attributed from what is owned, what is proposed from what is demonstrated, and what a model can estimate from what a legitimate institution may ultimately decide.

The framework becomes stronger not by collapsing these distinctions, but by making them explicit.

Purpose of this hub

The place where the framework states its own rules of reasoning.

The PBS/MPS research spans economic memory, performance measurement, contribution decomposition, ownership, institutional governance, procedural rights, public policy, and theories of justice.

Core Logic sits across those subjects. The pages collected here examine the transitions between them: what evidence supports a claim, how causal attribution is distinguished from participation, when an economic allocation requires an independent legal or institutional warrant, what governance protects the record, and what evidence would require the theory to narrow or fail.

Core Logic asks:

  • What kind of claim is this?
  • What evidence supports it?
  • What inference is being made?
  • Where does uncertainty enter?
  • Who has authority to decide?
  • What would falsify the application?
  • What consequence, if any, follows?
Canonical discipline

Evidence should precede entitlement. A record is not a right. Performance is not attribution. Attribution is not ownership. Mathematics does not manufacture a legal obligation. A consequential decision requires a warrant appropriate to the institution making it.

The Core Logic

Six questions at the framework’s most important joints.

These are not supplemental pages. They make explicit the evidentiary, causal, institutional, theoretical, and falsifiability rules that govern how stronger claims may emerge from the broader research.

Core Logic 01 Epistemic Status

Evidence Status

What exactly is being claimed, and what supports it?

Distinguishes external evidence, documented fact, author synthesis, hypothesis, normative argument, proposed architecture, mathematical model, and empirical demonstration. The objective is to prevent a proposal from being presented as validation or an inferential model from being presented as observation.

Core Logic 02 Inferential Boundary

Attribution and Ownership

What can contribution evidence establish before law or agreement intervenes?

Separates provenance, factual participation, performance, causal attribution, allocation, ownership, entitlement, and payment. A reliable record can establish historically important facts without automatically creating title, a royalty, a lien, a security, or another enforceable right.

Core Logic 03 Falsifiability

Failure Conditions

Under what conditions must an application narrow, stop, or fail?

Defines the evidence that should force revision or rejection: unreliable contribution identity, invalid baselines, unacceptable causal uncertainty, uncontainable privacy costs, metric gaming, uncorrectable records, institutional capture, excessive administration, or consequences stronger than the evidence can support.

Core Logic 06 Normative Theory

Rawls and Nozick Reconsidered

Can structural fairness and historical entitlement operate over a shared evidentiary record?

A restrained philosophical inquiry into whether provenance changes the evidentiary conditions under which competing theories of justice operate. The argument does not require those theories to become identical. It asks whether both benefit when acquisition, contribution, circumstances, transfer, dependence, and institutional conditions are remembered more completely.

Core analytical architecture

The architecture beneath the logic.

The framework is clearest when its analytical functions are separated from the market infrastructure that may later act on their outputs.

ANALYTICAL LAYER 01

Provenance Ledger

Establishes the evidentiary record of who contributed what, when, and in what context, together with the relevant dependencies, status, corrections, and evidentiary history.

ANALYTICAL LAYER 02

Performance Oracle

Supplies independently observable, domain-appropriate performance evidence concerning the outcomes associated with a documented asset, system, or contribution set.

ANALYTICAL LAYER 03

Contribution Decomposition & Royalty Flow

Relates documented contributions to measured outcomes, estimates attributable contribution under the applicable model, applies an allocation rule where justified, and supports the economic-participation logic specified for the implementation.

Analytical sequence

Provenance What happened?
Performance What outcome occurred?
Attribution / Allocation How do inputs relate to that outcome?
Entitlement / Payment What valid rule creates the consequence?
Exchange Protocol: surrounding market infrastructure where applicable. Listing, pricing, transfer, settlement, reporting, and auditability do not replace the analytical stages that precede market handling.

Why this belongs at the core

The framework has several kinds of validity to establish.

A technically feasible provenance mechanism would still be inadequate if its evidence were unreliable, its attribution invalid, its economics uneconomic, its governance captured, or its consequences unsupported by law or legitimate institutional authority.

Epistemic

Is the claim known, inferred, hypothesized, or proposed?

Evidence status determines how strongly a proposition may be stated and what additional evidence is required.

Causal

Did the contribution materially affect the outcome?

Attribution requires more than participation. It requires defensible treatment of baselines, marginal effects, interaction, persistence, substitution, and uncertainty.

Economic

Is remembering the contribution worth the cost?

Institutional memory is productive only where its decision value exceeds the full cost of recording, validation, measurement, privacy, governance, contestation, and administration.

Institutional

Can the system exercise power legitimately?

Consequential systems require purpose limitation, correction, contestability, independent review, procedural standing, and safeguards against capture.
