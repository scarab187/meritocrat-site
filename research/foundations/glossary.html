Glossary

Core terms used across the Meritocrat.org research network, including the normative, institutional, technical, and regulatory concepts used in the MPS/PBS framework.

Reading note: Terms identified as proposed describe concepts, models, or architecture within the research framework. Their inclusion here does not establish legal status, regulatory approval, technical validation, or empirical consensus.

Foundational Concepts

Provenance
The traceable causal history of an economic outcome or contribution, including the inputs that helped produce it.
Meritocratic Provenance
The portion of an outcome's causal history attributed within the framework to agency-related inputs such as effort, cultivated skill, innovation, creativity, judgment, or investment in one's own capacities.
Non-Meritocratic Provenance
The portion of an outcome's causal history attributed within the framework to morally arbitrary or antecedent conditions such as inherited resources, network position, geographic circumstance, or institutional favoritism.
Meritocratic Legitimacy
The proposed normative standard under which rewards should reasonably track relevant contribution and the background conditions of competition should be sufficiently fair for those contributions to be meaningfully attributable to agency.
Weak Meritocracy
The proposition that rewards track measured performance without asking how the capacity to perform was produced.
Strong Meritocracy
The stronger proposition that rewards should track performance that is, as far as practicable, attributable to agency rather than merely to antecedent advantage.
Advantage Laundering
The proposed process by which morally arbitrary or structural advantages pass through legitimate market processes and emerge as outcomes that appear wholly attributable to individual merit.
Provenance Correction
The proposed practice of identifying and responding to non-meritocratic inputs or their effects rather than treating inequality itself as the sole object of correction.

MPS Institutional Framework

Meritocratic Provenance System (MPS)
Proposed institutional framework for tracing, assessing, and correcting non-meritocratic inputs while preserving market information and incentives. Its analytical components include Provenance Auditing, Advantage Indexing, Corrective Redistribution, and Incentive Preservation.
Provenance Auditing
Proposed systematic assessment of the causal inputs to an economic outcome, expressed probabilistically rather than as a claim of perfect causal reconstruction.
Advantage Indexing
Proposed process for quantifying the degree of non-meritocratic advantage identified through provenance assessment.
Advantage Index
Proposed estimate of the proportion of an economic return attributable to non-meritocratic inputs.
Corrective Redistribution
The proposed MPS component that translates provenance assessments into institutional corrections, with emphasis where practicable on changing the processes that produce advantage rather than simply equalizing outcomes after the fact.
Incentive Preservation
The MPS design constraint that corrections should target non-meritocratic inputs while preserving rewards for genuine productive contribution and the informational properties of market processes.
Epistemic Humility
Design principle requiring uncertainty in provenance or contribution estimates to be acknowledged and reflected in the strength of resulting inferences or actions.
Counterfactual Problem
The measurement problem of estimating what an outcome would have been in the absence of a particular contribution, advantage, or other causal input.

PBS Technical Architecture

Provenance-Backed Securities (PBS)
Proposed instrument layer linking economic claims to provenance-verified contributions or assets and associated performance information. The term describes the framework's proposed instrument concept and does not itself establish a legal classification.
Provenance Ledger
Proposed evidentiary record layer for documenting contributions and associated identity, timestamp, evidence, and provenance context.
Performance Oracle
Proposed measurement layer that ingests domain-specific performance evidence and produces information that can participate in contribution valuation.
Contribution
Within the technical framework, an input considered for attribution when it is identifiable to a contributor, materially affects an observed outcome, and has modeled persistence over time.
Contribution Space
The modeled set of contributions considered by the contribution-decomposition framework.
Contribution Decomposition
Proposed analytical process connecting documented contributions with measurable outcomes by estimating marginal, interacting, and time-dependent contribution value.
Marginal Contribution
The change in modeled value associated with the presence of a contribution relative to a specified set of other contributions.
Approximate Marginal Contribution
An estimate of marginal contribution obtained when exact removal or isolation is impractical, including approaches that measure performance changes after a contribution is masked, perturbed, or down-weighted.
Persistence
The modeled continuation of a contribution's influence over time.
Persistence Function
A proposed mathematical representation of how a contribution's influence continues or decays over time.
Synergy
Additional modeled value associated with contributions acting together rather than separately.
Royalty Flow
Proposed mechanism for translating modeled contribution allocations into payments or economic distributions to contributors.
Budget Balance
Constraint within the proposed royalty model requiring aggregate royalty allocations not to exceed total modeled value unless an explicit external funding or subsidy mechanism is introduced.
Cryptographic Anchoring
Proposed use of cryptographic hashes and digital signatures to make later modification of records detectable and to associate records with verified identities. Record integrity does not by itself establish the truth of the original assertion.

Market and Incentive Concepts

Price Signal
Market information conveyed through prices about relative scarcity, demand, and valuation. The MPS framework treats the preservation of price signals as a design constraint rather than proposing centrally determined market prices.
Voluntary Exchange
Exchange entered by informed participants without compulsion. The framework adopts a presumption in favor of voluntary exchange while allowing the provenance of bargaining positions and productive capacities to remain an object of analysis.
Transaction Costs
Costs associated with activities such as measurement, information gathering, administration, contracting, and enforcement. The MPS framework recognizes provenance assessment and administration as sources of such costs.

Legal and Regulatory Concepts

Contribution Right
A proposed economic or legal interest associated with a documented contribution and the value attributed to it. Within the public research framework, the term should not be read as an already established property right absent an applicable contract, statute, regulation, or binding legal determination.
Washington Risk Capital Test
A Washington securities-law framework referenced in the project's regulatory analysis under RCW 21.20.005(17)(a). The project materials analyze investment or other consideration, risk capital, expectation of valuable benefit, and practical and actual managerial control. Application to any particular PBS instrument is fact-specific.
Practical and Actual Managerial Control
The managerial-control concept relevant to the project's analysis of Washington's risk-capital test. The project materials treat voting rights, operational authority, management replacement, transaction vetoes, collective action, protocol authority, and control of distribution structures as potentially relevant facts.
Regulatory Classification
The legal characterization of a specific instrument under applicable law. The Meritocrat.org research framework does not treat publication of the PBS concept as establishing a final securities or other regulatory classification.
