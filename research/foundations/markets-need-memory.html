Abstract

Modern production is cumulative, intangible, distributed, and increasingly machine-mediated. This paper argues that legitimate markets need governed institutions of economic memory, while rejecting automatic ownership, compulsory recording, universal tokenization, or a score of human worth.

Where cumulative and machine-mediated production makes contribution history materially relevant, society should preserve that history before deciding what rights or compensation, if any, should follow.

The structural mismatch

Value is created through networks of people, institutions, knowledge, data, software, capital, and machines, while ownership systems are designed to identify a terminal legal holder.

Why artificial intelligence raises the stakes

AI can make upstream contributions more economically consequential while making contributors less visible. The public-interest need is governable evidence, not automatic compensation.

What economic memory must do

A legitimate system must be relevant, verifiable, contestable, privacy-preserving, reversible, independent, proportionate, and valid for the domain.

A bounded research program

The proposal calls for comparative institutional analysis, adversarial testing, bounded pilots, independent replication, and explicit stopping rules.
