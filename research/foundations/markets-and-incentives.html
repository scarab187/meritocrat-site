Foundation 4

Markets and Incentives

The framework treats price signals, voluntary exchange, and productive incentives as constraints that a provenance system should preserve rather than replace.

Why preserve prices?

The comparative-theory manuscript accepts the informational role of market prices and treats the knowledge problem as a serious objection to centralized allocation. MPS therefore presents provenance auditing as input verification and structural correction, not as a system for centrally setting prices.

Incentive preservation

One of the four stated MPS components is Incentive Preservation. Proposed corrections are aimed at non-meritocratic inputs rather than suppressing returns to genuine productive effort.

Voluntary exchange

The framework adopts a presumption in favor of informed voluntary exchange while arguing that the provenance of bargaining positions and productive capacities can still be examined.

Transaction costs

The source material acknowledges that provenance auditing, measurement, and administration impose transaction costs. A deployable system would therefore need to demonstrate that information and legitimacy benefits exceed those costs.

Status: Design objective and theoretical argument. Empirical incentive effects would require testing.
