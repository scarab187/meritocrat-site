Foundation 6

Policy, Law and Regulation

PBS and MPS intersect with questions of securities regulation, property and intellectual property, contracts, privacy, data governance, institutional authority, governance design, and regulatory oversight. The first rule of this page is to keep proposed architecture separate from established law.

Public status rule
  1. Proposal: what the PBS/MPS architecture is designed to do.
  2. Author analysis: a legal or regulatory interpretation contained in project research.
  3. Regulator statement: what an agency actually communicated.
  4. Legal determination: a classification or obligation established through the legally applicable authority and facts.

These categories are not interchangeable. A proposal does not become law because it appears in a technical architecture, and an author analysis does not become an agency position because it was submitted to a regulator.

The regulatory perimeter

PBS/MPS does not present a single legal question. The architecture can touch several legal domains because it combines provenance records, performance measurement, contribution attribution, economic interests, identity information, contractual relationships, and potential market infrastructure.

Securities

Whether a specific instrument or transaction falls within applicable securities definitions and, if so, what registration, exemption, disclosure, or intermediary rules apply.

Property and IP

Whether a claimed contribution interest corresponds to an existing property or intellectual-property right, a contractual interest, or a proposed new legal category.

Contracts

How parties define contribution scope, attribution, economic participation, disputes, and enforceable obligations.

Privacy and data governance

How identity, provenance evidence, performance information, consent, access, and other regulated data are handled.

Core questions

What is PBS?

Proposed architecture

Provenance-Backed Securities are the proposed instrument layer associated with the broader Meritocratic Provenance System. Project materials connect economic interests with documented contributions, provenance information, performance evidence, attribution, and royalty logic.

That architectural description does not itself establish what the instrument is under applicable law.

Is PBS necessarily a security?

Not finally determined here

No final classification is asserted on this page. Regulatory treatment depends on the actual instrument and transaction, including its terms, participants, governance rights, economic structure, offering method, and applicable jurisdiction.

What did Washington DFI actually say?

Regulator statement

In the correspondence supplied with this research project, Washington DFI said that the contemplated PBS/MPS architecture appeared to go beyond the laws administered by DFI and could raise other legal issues, including property rights and intellectual property.

DFI further explained that, to the extent the architecture involves an offer or sale of securities, existing state and federal securities requirements would apply.

DFI did not choose an appropriate regulatory pathway for bringing PBS to market. It stated that this determination would need to be made by the company, ideally after consultation with legal counsel.

Why do both Howey and Washington's risk-capital test matter?

Regulatory context

DFI's correspondence states that the project's initial materials included a Howey analysis and that, during the regulatory discussions, DFI explained that a Washington securities analysis should also include review of the risk-capital test.

The project subsequently developed its own risk-capital analysis. That analysis is an author-side regulatory analysis and should not be represented as a classification made by DFI.

Why does governance matter?

Author analysis

The project's risk-capital analysis treats practical and actual managerial control as a governance-dependent question. The analysis examines matters such as voting rights, management replacement, operational authority, vetoes over material transactions, collective-action mechanisms, protocol upgrades, and authority over fees or distributions.

Those factors become legally relevant only through the applicable law and the facts of the actual governance arrangement.

What is the proposed Performance Oracle?

Proposed architecture

The Performance Oracle is the proposed empirical measurement layer. Project materials describe it as taking domain-specific performance evidence and producing information used by the broader valuation and attribution architecture.

Related design materials describe independent verification, anomaly detection, cross-validation, recalibration, and logged correction processes as safeguards around measurement.

What does cryptographic anchoring prove?

Technical distinction

In the proposed design, cryptographic hashes and signatures are intended to make later alteration detectable and associate records with specified identities or submission events.

Record integrity is not the same question as factual truth. A tamper-evident record can preserve an inaccurate, incomplete, disputed, or legally insufficient assertion. External evidence, validation, challenge, review, and correction procedures therefore remain necessary.

Is a “contribution right” already an established property right?

Proposed legal concept

No established universal property status is asserted here. PBS/MPS materials use contribution rights as part of the proposed architecture and discuss contractual and legislative paths for recognizing economic interests connected to contributions.

Public materials should therefore describe a contribution right as proposed unless a particular contract, statute, regulation, judgment, or other binding authority establishes the relevant legal interest.

Does pseudonymity eliminate identity and privacy obligations?

Proposed architecture

No. The design materials contemplate verified identity that need not necessarily be publicly disclosed. That design objective does not determine which privacy, identity, financial-regulation, contractual, or sector-specific requirements apply to a production implementation.

How are attribution and measurement disputes treated?

Proposed process

Project materials contemplate challenge, review, recalculation or recalibration, updated distributions, and preservation of dispute history in the audit trail.

These mechanisms describe intended architecture. They should not be presented as an existing statutory or agency dispute-resolution process.

Is the proposed architecture already deployed?

Development status

The supplied formal-submission materials describe PBS/MPS as being at an architectural and intellectual-property development stage at the time of that submission.

Unless a particular implementation is independently established, public Research Foundations pages should therefore describe the Ledger, Oracle, Contribution Decomposition, royalty, identity, governance, and examination functions as proposed design features.

The regulatory record is part of the evidence

Project submissions and regulator responses should remain separate source categories. That separation prevents a proposal submitted to an agency from later being repeated as though the agency adopted it.

  1. Project proposes. Technical, economic, mathematical, and legal materials describe PBS/MPS and make project-side arguments.
  2. Agency responds. The agency's own correspondence establishes what the agency actually said.
  3. Public research distinguishes them. Meritocrat.org can explain both without merging their authority.
  4. Binding status remains fact-specific. A research page should not manufacture a legal determination where the available record does not contain one.

What the DFI correspondence establishes

  • DFI reviewed materials supplied in connection with PBS/MPS.
  • DFI said the architecture appeared to extend beyond the laws administered by DFI and could raise other legal issues.
  • DFI identified property-right and intellectual-property questions among those broader issues.
  • DFI said securities requirements apply to the extent an offer or sale of securities is involved.
  • DFI indicated that both Howey and Washington's risk-capital test should be considered in securities analysis.
  • DFI did not select the regulatory pathway for bringing PBS to market.
  • DFI recommended engaging competent legal counsel given the proposal's complexity.

What the regulatory record does not establish

The supplied correspondence should not be represented as an endorsement of PBS/MPS, a determination that the architecture is technically valid, a final securities classification, approval of a contribution-right theory, or selection of a path to market.

Legal boundary: This page organizes research and source distinctions. It does not determine the classification of a security, establish a property right, authorize an offering, or substitute for transaction-specific legal analysis.

The legal logic layer

Within the Research Foundations circuit, law sits downstream from provenance and measurement. Before an institution can decide what legal consequence should attach to a contribution, the framework first has to identify what was contributed, determine what evidence supports the record, and estimate what relationship the contribution has to the relevant outcome.

Law then asks a different question: what legal significance, if any, follows from those facts under the applicable authority?

Do not collapse the layers: provenance establishes an evidentiary claim; measurement estimates an effect; attribution assigns analytical significance; policy proposes a response; law determines which rights, duties, limits, classifications, and procedures actually apply.
