Purpose of the series

Principles must be able to become institutions.

The Foundations Series asks what must be established before contribution evidence can become consequential. It proceeds from evidence to economics, from economics to public legitimacy, and from institutional power to the rights of the person recorded by the system.

The vision is reciprocal rather than adversarial. Enterprise retains its role in coordinating capital, risk, people, and markets. Contributors gain a credible, portable, contestable record and a lawful path to recognition where evidence and an independent warrant justify it.

Work and capital should not stand as opposing forces when each depends upon the other. The objective is a stronger compact built on evidence, reciprocity, responsibility, and human dignity.

Canonical sequence

No entitlement without the steps that justify it.

1. Provenance
What happened, who contributed, and what evidence supports the record?
2. Performance
What observable outcome occurred under valid measures and baselines?
3. Attribution / Allocation
How do documented inputs relate to outcomes under disclosed uncertainty?
4. Entitlement / Payment
What independent rule creates an enforceable consequence?

Paper 1 available

Evidence Before Entitlement

Provenance as an Institution of Economic Memory

Economic justice is often asked to decide too much from too little. This paper treats provenance as governed economic memory: evidence of who contributed what, when, under which conditions, and through which dependencies. It insists that provenance does not itself establish desert, ownership, entitlement, allocation, or payment.

Paper 2 available

The Economics of Institutional Memory

Why Markets Record Possession Better Than Contribution

Markets preserve title, balances, transfers, and prices with exceptional precision, but contribution is a costly causal history. This paper asks when credible, portable contribution records improve coordination enough to justify recording, validation, attribution, correction, privacy, governance, and administration costs.

Paper 3 available

Markets Need Memory

Human Contribution, Artificial Intelligence, and the Institutional Conditions of Legitimate Markets

Modern production is cumulative, intangible, distributed, and increasingly machine-mediated. This paper argues that legitimate markets need governed institutions of economic memory, while rejecting automatic ownership, compulsory recording, universal tokenization, or a score of human worth.

Paper 4 available

The Rights of the Recorded Contributor

Notice, Consent, Attribution, Correction, Appeal, and Exit

A provenance system can become a surveillance system when the person it describes cannot know, inspect, challenge, correct, limit, or leave the process. This paper proposes a bill of procedural rights that protects the contributor without predetermining any substantive outcome.

From declaration to action

One project, expressed through research, platform design, public policy, and participation.

The Foundations Series is the connective tissue. It disciplines the movement’s claims before those claims become technical architecture, policy proposals, institutional pilots, contractual standards, or legal arguments.

Restoring the equation

Employers need room to organize, invest, and accept risk. Employees and other contributors need a record that does not disappear into institutional ownership. Balance is restored by improving evidence, bargaining conditions, procedural standing, and lawful participation, not by pretending that every contribution is automatically property.
