PBS/MPS Research

Public research, architecture, policy materials, and regulatory context supporting the development of Provenance-Backed Securities and the Meritocratic Provenance System.

Why maintain a public research library?

PBS/MPS combines questions from economics, securities regulation, provenance, intangible assets, artificial intelligence, intellectual property, performance measurement, and contribution attribution.

Meritocrat.org separates public research from advocacy so that legislators, researchers, regulators, technical reviewers, and interested members of the public can examine the foundations of the proposal directly.

This library also distinguishes between research that has been released publicly and work that remains under technical, regulatory, legal, or publication review.

Architecture Clarification

One framework, three functional views

The architecture descriptions are not competing definitions when each is labeled by function. They describe the same broader PBS/MPS program at different layers.

Market Infrastructure

PBS market infrastructure

Provenance Ledger + Performance Oracle + Exchange Protocol

The public market-facing view: record contributions, evaluate performance, and support exchange of resulting economic interests.

Analytical Architecture

PBS/MPS analytical architecture

Provenance Ledger + Performance Oracle + Contribution Decomposition & Royalty Flow

The valuation and allocation view: document inputs, observe outcomes, estimate attributable contribution, and calculate proposed royalty flows.

Institutional Framework

MPS institutional framework

Provenance Auditing + Advantage Indexing + Corrective Redistribution + Incentive Preservation

The broader economic and policy view: identify non-meritocratic inputs, measure them, design corrections, and preserve market information and incentives.

Terminology rule: Use “PBS market infrastructure,” “PBS/MPS analytical architecture,” or “MPS institutional framework” before listing components. Do not call all three lists “the architecture” without a layer label.
Research Status

Work under technical review

Some PBS/MPS research exists in more detailed mathematical, technical, legal, or regulatory form than is currently published on Meritocrat.org.

The existence of such work should not be interpreted as meaning that every draft, model, formula, implementation choice, or analysis has been approved for public release.

Technical materials may be revised, independently reviewed, versioned, or released separately when appropriate for scholarly, legislative, regulatory, or technical examination.

Architecture and implementation status: Unless a page expressly states otherwise and identifies supporting evidence, functions described on Meritocrat.org are proposed design features rather than claims of production deployment, independent technical validation, regulatory approval, or demonstrated performance at scale. Cryptographic anchoring can make later alteration detectable, but it does not by itself establish that an original assertion, identity claim, performance input, attribution result, or valuation is accurate. Those questions require external evidence, verification, audit, challenge procedures, and continuing review.
Mathematical Research

Contribution Decomposition research

ScarabCloud has developed a formal mathematical treatment of Contribution Decomposition and Royalty Flow within the PBS/MPS research program.

The research examines the problem of relating identifiable contributions to measurable outcomes in systems where contributions can interact, persist over time, and participate in collaborative value creation.

Regulatory Record

Research and regulatory engagement

ScarabCloud engaged with the Washington State Department of Financial Institutions during development of the PBS/MPS proposal.

During that engagement, the materials described to DFI included the Provenance Ledger, Performance Oracle, and Contribution Decomposition & Royalty Flow architecture.

DFI subsequently explained that the proposed architecture appeared to extend beyond the laws administered by DFI and could raise issues including property rights and intellectual property.

DFI did not endorse PBS/MPS or provide a regulatory pathway for bringing PBS to market.

How does Meritocrat.org distinguish research from policy?

Research can describe a model, investigate an economic problem, develop an architecture, or test an analytical framework.

Policy asks whether and how institutions should act on those findings.

Meritocrat.org therefore treats technical research, ScarabCloud's policy conclusions, proposed legislation, and statements made by regulators as distinct categories.

Publication of research on Meritocrat.org does not imply adoption of the research by DFI, another regulator, a legislature, a court, an academic institution, or an independent reviewer.
